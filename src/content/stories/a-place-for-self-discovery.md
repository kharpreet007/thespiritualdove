There is a particular kind of journey that has very little to do with distance. You may not travel far at all, and yet something shifts. A temple courtyard, a riverbank at dusk, a lane where chanting drifts out of open doors: the place asks nothing of you, and in that space you begin to hear yourself.

## Leaving the usual voices behind

At home, our days are crowded with expectations: work, messages, plans, the small roles we play for different people. Travel loosens them. In an unfamiliar place nobody knows what you are supposed to be, and that can feel unsettling at first, then surprisingly light.

> Sometimes we have to leave the familiar to notice what we were carrying.

## What a place can offer

Some places are quiet enough to let you think. Others are so full of devotion and song that your own worries seem to soften beside them. Neither is better. What matters is that the place gives you something to lean against while you look inward.

## Questions worth bringing along

You do not need answers on the way, only a few good questions. What do I notice when I stop rushing? What am I grateful for here? What would I like to carry back home with me?

## Carrying it home

The real work of self-discovery begins after the journey ends. The place was only the mirror. What you saw in it, and what you choose to do about it, is yours to keep.
