intro: >-
  Amritsar, in the north-western Indian state of Punjab, is best known for Harmandir Sahib, widely called the Golden
  Temple, the holiest gurdwara of Sikhism. It stands in the middle of a sacred pool, the Amrit Sarovar, and is reached
  by a causeway from the surrounding marble walkway. Around it, the old city is a lively mix of bazaars, food stalls
  and memorials.
history: >-
  Harmandir Sahib was established in the late 16th century under the guidance of the Sikh Gurus, and the city of
  Amritsar grew around it. The upper floors were later covered in gold, giving the temple its familiar name. Amritsar
  is also closely tied to the history of the 1919 Jallianwala Bagh massacre and to the Partition of India in 1947.
significance: >-
  The temple has entrances on all four sides, often understood as a sign of openness to people from every direction
  and background. Its langar, a free community kitchen, serves meals to visitors regardless of faith, caste or status.

highlights:
  - title: Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple)
    text: The heart of the city, open to visitors of every faith at almost any hour.
  - title: The parikrama
    text: The marble walkway around the sarovar, best experienced slowly, especially early or late in the day.
  - title: Langar
    text: A shared meal served to everyone who sits down, prepared largely by volunteers.
  - title: Gurbani
    text: Continuous devotional singing that carries across the water.
  - title: The temple at night
    text: The illuminated gurdwara and its reflection create a very different atmosphere after dark.
  - title: Jallianwala Bagh
    text: A memorial garden a few minutes' walk from the temple, remembering those killed in 1919.
  - title: Partition Museum
    text: A museum near the Town Hall that gathers personal stories and objects from the 1947 Partition.
  - title: Wagah–Attari border ceremony
    text: A daily flag-lowering ceremony at the India–Pakistan border, roughly 30 km from the city. It is loud and very crowded.

transport:
  - mode: air
    title: By air
    text: Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee International Airport is roughly 11–13 km from the temple, about 30 minutes by taxi depending on traffic.
  - mode: rail
    title: By train
    text: Amritsar Junction is about 2 km from the temple and is connected to Delhi and many other cities, including fast daytime trains from New Delhi.
  - mode: road
    title: By road
    text: Amritsar is connected by national highway to Delhi, Chandigarh and Jammu. Regular buses run to the main inter-state bus terminal.
  - mode: local
    title: Getting around
    text: Auto-rickshaws, e-rickshaws and cycle-rickshaws are easy to find. The final approach along Heritage Street is pedestrian, so expect to walk the last stretch.

etiquette:
  - Cover your head before entering the complex and keep it covered throughout.
  - Remove shoes and socks, and wash your feet at the entrance pools.
  - Tobacco, alcohol and intoxicants are not permitted.
  - Sit on the floor during langar and accept what is served with respect.
  - Follow guidance from sevadars (volunteers) about where photography is appropriate.
