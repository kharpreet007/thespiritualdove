intro: >-
  Vrindavan is a small town in the Mathura district of Uttar Pradesh, on the banks of the Yamuna. It is filled with
  temples, ashrams and bhajan halls, and from early morning its lanes echo with chanting and the greeting "Radhe Radhe".
history: >-
  Vrindavan has been a place of pilgrimage for centuries. From the 16th century it grew into a major centre of the
  Bhakti movement, when saints and devotees came here to identify and restore places linked to Krishna's life.
  Temples from that period, such as Govind Dev, stand alongside much newer ones built in recent decades.
significance: >-
  For devotees of Krishna, Vrindavan is among the most sacred places on earth — the forest land where Krishna is
  believed to have spent his childhood and youth with Radha and the gopis. Many visitors come to take darshan, walk
  the parikrama around the town, and take part in kirtan and festivals.

  bestTime: October to March is generally the most comfortable. Holi (February–March) and Janmashtami (August–September) are spectacular but extremely crowded.
  timings: Temple darshan timings differ from temple to temple and change between summer and winter — check each temple's current schedule.

etiquette:
  - Dress modestly and remove footwear before entering temples.
  - Respect photography rules — some temples do not allow photos of the deity.
  - Keep a firm hold of glasses, phones and bags; monkeys in Vrindavan are known for snatching them.
  - Move patiently in crowds, especially at Banke Bihari and during festivals.
  - Greet people with "Radhe Radhe", as locals do.
  - Do not waste water or leave plastic near the Yamuna and the ghats.
